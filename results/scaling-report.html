Nodes, Not Cores

Measuring what actually limits parallelism in community-partitioned GNN training on Spark — across 4, 8 and 12 EMR nodes.

The pipeline had shown almost no speedup from added hardware: on reddit, quadrupling the executors bought 1.12×. The question was whether that reflects a limit of the method or a limit of how it was being run. It turned out to be neither — it was a property of the benchmark and of the hardware's real topology.

Why more executors didn't help

Adding executors to a fixed set of nodes barely moved the wall clock, and the per-task numbers show why: each task simply got slower. Per-task median time is a clean function of how many tasks share a node.

Per-node throughput is roughly constant. Median task time approximately doubles each time task density doubles, so extra slots subdivide a fixed per-node capacity rather than adding capacity. The binding resource is per-node memory and I/O bandwidth — which is why adding machines works and adding slots does not.

The hardware was half the size it appeared

The instances are r6id.8xlarge, reporting 32 vCPUs. They have 16 physical cores with two threads each. Running 32 single-threaded tasks per node therefore oversubscribes the cores 2×, and the second hyperthread returns roughly 11% rather than 100%. Efficiency computed against vCPU counts overstates it by a factor of two.

Giving each task both hyperthreads instead — half the tasks, two threads each — was measurably better: 224.8s versus 231.4s on half the executors.

Usable parallelism — total task time divided by the longest single task — is the most slots a stage can ever benefit from, because it cannot finish before its slowest unit does. For reddit that ceiling is 36, well under the 64 physical cores already available. Extra hardware buys idle cores.

Every partitioner lands in the same place: Louvain 23.6, Leiden 23.5, LPA 22.4, igraph-LPA 23.8 (computed on member counts). LPA produces ten times as many communities — 220 against Louvain's 22 — but its largest community is 181,907 nodes, nearly four times Louvain's, and only ten survive the minimum-size filter. The bound is set by the largest unit, not the number of them.

Findings

  1. Throughput scales with nodes, not cores

    2.17× for 3× the nodes. Holding slots fixed at 128 and only spreading wider gave 143.7s → 116.4s, a 1.23× gain from placement alone. Doubling slots on fixed nodes gave about 1.10×.

  2. Task density, not core count, predicts per-task cost

    Median task time runs 12.2s at 4.6 tasks/node and 37.8s at 32 — monotonic across all 18 runs. This is the diagnostic that separates a genuine ceiling (flat median, fixed maximum) from oversubscription (rising median).

  3. The cost driver reverses between datasets

    On reddit, cost correlates with training-node count (+0.52) and not at all with edges (−0.01). On ogbn-products it is the opposite: edges +0.62, training nodes −0.02. No single balancing heuristic can be correct for both — a train-aware split that cut skew from 3.51× to 1.50× still made reddit 10.6% slower.

  4. Phase 3 and Phase 3b have different optima

    On 12 nodes Phase 3 is fastest at 192 slots (103.8s) while Phase 3b is fastest at 128 (182.5s vs 204.3s). Total wall clock is therefore lowest at 128 slots, not at maximum scale.

  5. A serial floor caps any achievable speedup

    Phase 3 carries roughly 40–55s of work that never parallelises — Delta load, broadcast, collect. At 12 nodes the parallel portion is about 91s against 103.8s measured, so further hardware has progressively less to act on.

What this means for the paper

A simple "distributed is faster" claim is not supported by reddit at any cluster size, and cannot be. But the finding that replaces it is stronger: we can now predict a workload's speedup ceiling before running it, explain the two distinct mechanisms that produce a flat curve, and show genuine scaling on a workload whose ceiling exceeds the hardware.

Scalability claims belong on ogbn-products and papers100M. reddit belongs in the paper as the case that demonstrates the ceiling.

Limits of this study

Phase 4 has never produced a number. It was gated off by a configuration condition for the whole study, so no retention figure against the single-machine full-graph baseline exists yet. That remains the largest gap.

Two timing bases. The tables above use the pipeline's own wall clock, which includes per-phase setup. The archived data also records Spark stage times, which run 10–13% lower (90.1s against 103.8s at 12 nodes). Both are correct measurements of different spans; speedup ratios agree closely either way (1.38× vs 1.44× for 8 → 12 nodes). They must never be mixed in one table.

Usable parallelism is basis-dependent too. Computed from Spark task times it reads 99–157 for ogbn-products; from the pipeline's internal timings it reads higher. The reddit figures (34.7–37.0) agree closely across bases, which is why that ceiling is quoted with confidence.

Ten epochs. These runs measure scaling, not accuracy. At 200 epochs reddit node accuracy is 0.8835 against 0.7347 here, while link AUC barely moves (0.8094 vs 0.8058) — the two tasks want very different budgets.
